Read every figure here as a candidate estimate, not a quote. Advertised tier rates are the starting point lenders publish; the rate in a letter of offer is set after credit assessment and is frequently negotiated below the advertised tier, particularly on the packaged products. LMI premiums are modelled from published indicative bands and vary by insurer, lender, loan purpose and borrower profile — treat them as an order of magnitude. Serviceability uses a single buffer on the product rate and a crude living-expense floor; real assessment applies each lender’s own expense benchmark, income shading and commitment treatment, so a lender’s answer will differ.

On stamp duty. The base duty scales were checked against an independent comparison at $500,000, $800,000 and $1,000,000 and reconcile exactly in all eight jurisdictions. Owner-occupier concessions are applied automatically where the state has one (Queensland’s home concession, Victoria’s principal-place-of-residence scale, the ACT’s eligible owner-occupier scale). First-home concessions are exact where the revenue office publishes a formula or table — Queensland and Western Australia — and are a straight-line taper elsewhere, flagged in the duty note as an approximation. Foreign-purchaser surcharges, off-the-plan concessions and regional variations are not modelled. Confirm duty with your state revenue office’s own calculator before relying on it.

Also excluded: offset balances, redraw, cashback and fee-waiver offers, and lenders’ valuation fees. Fixed-rate products revert to a variable rate at the end of the fixed term and the reversion rate is not modelled, so a fixed row’s full-term cost understates the real figure. Investment lending, interest-only repayments and company or trust borrowers are out of scope. Rates and duty scales move — confirm current numbers before acting.
